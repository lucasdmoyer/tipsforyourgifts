# Gift-Thread editorial cartoons

Status: founder-approved v1.0 for the publication-wide visual rollout on 2026-08-23.

## The visual idea

Show the recipient using the gift, not a catalog arrangement of products. A saffron thread or ribbon connects the useful roles in a thoughtful pair and makes the interaction understandable before the caption is read.

## Repeatable art direction

- Use a gentle three-quarter top-down viewpoint, simplified geometric forms, chunky dark-evergreen ink contours, soft gouache fills, subtle risograph misregistration, and recycled-paper grain.
- Use the site palette: parchment cream, deep evergreen, muted mint, saffron gold, restrained coral, and occasional dusty blue.
- Keep characters adult, expressive, and grounded in an observable recipient routine. Vary age, skin tone, hair, and home or outdoor context across the article set.
- Compose for a 3:2 landscape scene that remains legible at mobile width. Prefer one clear interaction and related gift roles.
- Omit embedded text by default. The article supplies precise product names and decision guidance.
- Illustrate generic product categories and their interaction. Never copy packaging, interfaces, logos, book covers, characters, maps, or proprietary artwork.
- Keep the image charming but editorial: warm, intelligent, lightly playful, and never glossy, photorealistic, childish, or cluttered.

## Product-card truth boundary

These are editorial context illustrations, not product photographs. Product cards label that distinction next to every image. Exact product identity, compatibility, drawbacks, merchant, and link live in the reviewed text, never inside the artwork.

## Publishing gate

Every accepted image needs a source checksum, optimized-file checksum, descriptive alt text, caption, product and pair coverage, and a responsive layout check. This rollout is bound to Lucas's explicit approval and deploy instruction; later style changes or third-party images require fresh approval.

## First approved scene

The first approved scene illustrates the `Sapiens` plus `Civilization VI` idea without reproducing either product. A reader moves between an open big-history book and a laptop showing an original hex-world; a saffron ribbon turns the two activities into one gift ritual.
